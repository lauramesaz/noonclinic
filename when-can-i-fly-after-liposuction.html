    Flying home safely

    When Can You Fly
    After Liposuction?

    Your flight home gets cleared by how you're healing, not by the days your calendar has free.

    International patients · noon Clinic team · Updated October 3, 2026

    You've got the lipo in Medellín on the calendar, you've already done the math on the days off work, and then the real question lands: when can you fly after liposuction? There's no single number that works for every lipo patient. It comes down to how you're healing, how long your flight is, and what your surgeon tells you at the follow-up. Here's what actually holds up, without rounding a number down just because it fits the ticket you're eyeing.

    What ASPS says about when you can fly after liposuction

    ASPS, the American Society of Plastic Surgeons, suggests waiting 5 to 7 days before flying after a body procedure like liposuction. That's a general reference, not a number we made up and not a green light that kicks in automatically on day six. It's a guideline built around one specific risk, not a rule that overrides how you're actually recovering.

    Wait before flying (ASPS)5 to 7 days after a body procedure
    Blood clot risk from immobilityStays elevated for 6 weeks
    Reference stay in Medellín2 to 3 weeks, body contouring

    Notice those are two different numbers. The 5 to 7 days is when it's reasonable to board a plane. The 6 weeks is how long the elevated clot risk from sitting still actually lasts, the same concern behind any long-haul flight. They don't cancel each other out: you can be inside that flying window and still need to move around, stay hydrated, and wear your compression garment on the plane, because the risk doesn't vanish on day eight.

    What happens at your follow-up before you're cleared

    Here's the part that matters most: those ranges are a starting point, not your flight date. Your surgeon confirms the real date at your follow-up, based on how your body is healing, not the ticket you already paid for or the days your manager approved.

    At noon, we work with a reference stay of 2 to 3 weeks for body contouring and combined surgeries. That's longer than ASPS's 5 to 7 days because it includes something that number doesn't: the in-person follow-ups you need before anyone clears you to fly. It's not caution for its own sake. It's the difference between "the days are up" and "we checked you and you're healing well."

    Booking a fixed return date before surgery usually ends in changing that ticket. Book something flexible instead, and lock in the exact day once you're in Medellín, after your first follow-up.

    A 3-hour flight and a 6-hour flight aren't the same trip

    From Miami, the nonstop to Medellín (MDE) runs about 3 hours 30 minutes. From Houston, about 4 hours 40 minutes. From New York, about 5 hours 30 minutes. No guideline sets a different waiting period by flight length, but common sense does: more hours stuck in a seat is more time with an active clot risk, so the longer your route, the more it's worth staying toward the back end of whatever window your surgeon gives you, not right at the earliest edge of it.

    If your route connects through Bogotá before the last short hop to Medellín, which is how it typically works from Atlanta, Boston or Tampa, that extra leg counts toward your total hours seated too, not just the final domestic segment. One more thing worth knowing if you've never been: Medellín's airport, José María Córdova (MDE), actually sits in Rionegro, outside the city, so the drive in and out adds to a travel day that's already long.

    That extra leg counts too.

    What you can control once you're on the plane

    Nobody expects you to stand for six hours. But a few things help: keep your compression garment on, get up and walk the aisle roughly every hour, and drink water instead of running on airport coffee. These lower a general risk that applies to any long flight after surgery. They're not a workaround for flying earlier than your surgeon has actually cleared you to.

    And here's the honest part we'd rather say upfront: if your job only gives you five or six days off total, flight time included, liposuction isn't the right procedure for that trip. That's not enough margin for ASPS's minimum, let alone a real follow-up visit. In that situation, the fix is moving the trip, not compressing the recovery.

    How this plays out in practice

    Before you fixate on flight dates, it's worth reading about safety (that page is in Spanish, like most of noon's site): a licensed operating room and an in-person consultation aren't optional, no matter how many days you stay.

    First comes an in-person consultation in Medellín, with your pre-op tests in order. Then the procedure itself, performed in Q2's licensed operating rooms. Then the follow-ups, which is where your surgeon actually checks swelling and healing before anyone talks about a flight date. That's when you walk away with a real date, not a guess made from your couch in the US before you've even landed.

    Frequently asked questions

    Frequently asked questions

    How soon can I fly after liposuction?

    As a general reference, ASPS (the American Society of Plastic Surgeons) suggests waiting 5 to 7 days before flying after a body procedure like liposuction. That's a starting point, not a flight date: your surgeon confirms the actual date at your follow-up, based on how you're healing.

    Does flight length matter, or just how many days have passed?

    Both. The concern behind the waiting period is blood clot risk from sitting still, and ASPS notes that risk stays elevated for the 6 weeks after surgery. A 6-hour flight keeps you immobile longer than a 3-hour one, so at the same number of recovery days, a longer route calls for more caution.

    Is there a different rule for short versus long flights?

    No official rule splits the waiting period by flight length. ASPS's 5-to-7-day guidance and the 6-week clot-risk window apply either way. It's just reasonable to be more cautious on a longer flight, inside whatever window your surgeon clears you for, rather than flying on the earliest possible day.

    What if I already booked a fixed return date?

    Change it if you need to. Booking a fixed return before surgery is the most common way to end up rebooking. We work with a reference stay of 2 to 3 weeks for body contouring, and confirm the exact day once you're in Medellín, at your follow-up.

    What can I actually do on the plane to lower the risk?

    The basics: wear your compression garment, get up and walk the aisle every so often, and drink water instead of relying on airport coffee. These are general precautions for any long flight after surgery, not a reason to move up a date your surgeon hasn't cleared yet.
