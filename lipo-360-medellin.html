    Before you decide

    What to know about lipo 360

    What lipo 360 is, and the names people search for

    Lipo 360, 360 liposuction and 360 liposculpture are names for the same thing: liposuction that treats your whole torso in a single surgery. In an article from the American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS), a member surgeon describes it as lipo of the abdomen done together with the flanks, treating the front and back of the torso. That's why it covers your abdomen, waist, flanks and back. The 360 isn't a technique or a device: it refers to the areas being treated.

    How it differs from lipo on a single area

    It's the same surgery with more areas, done through small incisions in the licensed operating rooms at Q2 in Medellín, never in a doctor's office. Liposuction takes 2 to 5 hours; your surgeon will tell you how long yours will take at your in-person consultation. More areas can mean more fat removed, and the ASPS notes that special considerations are needed when large amounts are suctioned: your health sets that limit. If your surgeon recommends it, you can add a fat transfer to your buttocks (BBL), a 3- to 5-hour surgery under general anesthesia.

    Who it's not for

    The ASPS describes a good candidate as an adult close to their ideal weight, with firm, elastic skin and good health, who doesn't smoke or vape and has clear goals. Lipo 360 isn't for you, or not right now, if:

    • What you're looking for is weight loss.
    • Your weight goes up and down.
    • What bothers you most is loose skin on your abdomen: lipo doesn't tighten it.
    • You have a condition that makes healing harder.
    • You smoke or vape and won't quit before surgery.

    Your surgeon makes the final call, with your pre-op test results.

    Risks you should know about

    Every surgery carries risks. For liposuction, the ASPS lists these, among others:

    • Anesthesia risks.
    • Bruising, swelling or fluid buildup.
    • Infection or poor wound healing.
    • Irregular contours, asymmetry or rippling.
    • Changes in skin sensation.
    • Deep vein thrombosis and heart or lung complications.

    The CDC reviewed deaths of US citizens after cosmetic surgery abroad and identified obesity and having several procedures in the same surgery as risk factors for embolism. That's why your surgeon decides what gets added to lipo 360, and we don't operate unless your pre-op tests are in order.

    How to prepare, and care afterward

    You get your pre-op tests, stop smoking or vaping and go over your medications with your specialist: the ASPS warns that aspirin, anti-inflammatory drugs and herbal supplements can increase bleeding. We tell you what to stop taking. An adult takes you home and stays with you the first night.

    The ASPS says the compression garment helps control swelling. The first week, you take short walks and have lymphatic drainage massages. If fat was also moved to your buttocks, you avoid direct pressure on them as your surgeon instructs. Your surgeon decides when you're cleared to fly after lipo.

    When another option makes more sense

    Some things lipo 360 doesn't fix.

    • If you have extra skin on your abdomen or diastasis recti: a tummy tuck, which is often combined with lipo, or a mini tummy tuck if the extra skin is minimal and below the belly button.
    • If only one area bothers you: liposuction of that area or, if you'd rather not have surgery, cryolipolysis (in Spanish).
    • If you want to lose weight: medical weight loss.
    • If your breasts and abdomen changed after pregnancy: a mommy makeover.

    At your consultation, we'll tell you what makes sense for you.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    What areas does lipo 360 include?

    Your abdomen, waist, flanks and back. Arms and thighs are separate areas. Your surgeon decides what's included in your case at your in-person consultation.

    Does lipo 360 replace a tummy tuck?

    No. It removes fat, but it doesn't remove extra skin or repair diastasis recti (separated abdominal muscles). That's what a tummy tuck is for, and it's often combined with lipo.

    Is lipo 360 different from regular liposuction?

    It's the same surgery, done on more areas: lipo 360 is liposuction that treats your whole torso, meaning your abdomen, waist, flanks and back, in a single surgery. You may also see it called 360 liposculpture. Liposculpture is another name for liposuction, one that puts the emphasis on shaping. Your surgeon decides which areas are included in your case at your in-person consultation.

    Is lipo 360 the same as a BBL?

    No. Lipo 360 removes fat from your abdomen, waist and back. A BBL, or Brazilian butt lift, takes fat out with liposuction, purifies it and moves it to your buttocks. They can be done in the same surgery, but then they're two procedures: whether to combine them is up to your surgeon at your in-person consultation, and it's confirmed with your pre-op test results.

    How long is lipo 360 recovery?

    You'll need about 2 to 3 weeks off work. In the first week, you take short walks and have lymphatic drainage massages; between weeks 2 and 4, you keep wearing the compression garment, continue the massages and get back to your routine. The swelling goes down completely between months 3 and 6. If it's combined with another surgery, your surgeon will confirm the timing.

    Is lipo 360 safe?

    Every procedure carries risks, and lipo does too: the ASPS lists anesthesia risks, deep vein thrombosis and irregular contours, among others. The US CDC identified obesity and having several procedures in the same surgery as risk factors for embolism. That's why we don't operate unless your pre-op tests are in order, and your surgeon decides what gets combined.
