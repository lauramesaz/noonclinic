What to pack for plastic surgery in Colombia: paperwork before clothes

Start with the boring part, because it's the one that actually derails a trip if you forget it. US citizens don't need a tourist visa: Colombian immigration grants a permit of up to 90 days, renewable up to 180 days a year, and medical treatment is listed among the activities a tourist is allowed to do under that permit. If you hold dual Colombian-US citizenship, the rule flips: you travel on your valid Colombian passport, because you enter and leave Colombia as a Colombian citizen.

Also worth doing before you fly: Colombia's Check-MIG, a free online form immigration recommends. And bring a copy of your recent pre-op test results, on your phone or on paper. Not because they replace the in-person consultation, which is required either way, but because your surgeon is going to want to see them from day one.

Pack for Medellín's weather, not your hometown's

This is the most common mistake for anyone flying in from a Boston or New York winter: packing the heavy coat. You won't need it. Medellín holds a nearly constant mild climate year-round, which is why locals call it "the city of eternal spring," with temperatures running roughly 63 to 82°F. Light layers, a thin jacket for the evening, and something water-resistant if your trip lands in rainy season do more work than any winter coat, which just takes up space you don't have.

Coming from Miami or Orlando, the adjustment runs the other way. Florida's humid heat isn't Medellín's climate. You'll need less than you think, plus something long-sleeved for cooler evenings.

Pack for recovery, not for sightseeing

This is where packing for surgery looks different from packing for a trip. Loose clothing that buttons or zips in front, nothing you'd have to pull over your head those first days. Shoes without laces you'd have to bend down for. A small travel pillow, if you'll be sitting for a while in the car or on the flight home.

Don't buy your compression garment ahead of time on your own. Your surgeon prescribes it based on the specific procedure, and it's often sized or handed to you once you're already in Medellín, when they know exactly what you need. Buying one in advance, guessing at the size, usually means buying it twice.

And here's the honest part worth saying upfront: nothing you pack replaces the in-person consultation or having your pre-op tests in order. You can show up with the perfect suitcase and still have the surgery postponed if something in your labs isn't right.

Packing well doesn't fix that.

Medication, chargers, and what you can leave behind

Bring your regular medications in their original packaging, along with a list of names and doses, in case your surgeon needs to check it. On electronics, good news: Colombia uses the same plug type and voltage as the US, so your phone charger, laptop and flat iron all work without an adapter.

What you can leave behind: the winter coat, the full-size hair dryer (it's heavy and you won't use it that much), and the "just in case" extra outfits that only add weight to a bag you'll be carrying with one hand, not two.

How this plays out in practice

Before you pack your final bag, confirm with your surgeon the tests they've ordered and your expected stay: 1 to 2 weeks for face or breast procedures, 2 to 3 weeks for body contouring or combined surgeries, as covered in how long to stay in Colombia after plastic surgery. That window tells you how much everyday clothing you need, on top of the recovery items.
