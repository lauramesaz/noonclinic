If you're thinking about getting plastic surgery in Colombia as a US resident, you've probably seen two kinds of stories online: glowing before-and-afters, and warnings about trips that went wrong. Both are real. The difference is usually in the details: where the surgery happens, who examines you, and how much time you give yourself in Medellin before flying home. This guide covers those details.

Safety first: what to look for in Medellin

We'll start here because it's the question that matters most. Every surgery has risks, including bleeding, infection, healing problems and anesthesia-related complications. No clinic, in Colombia or the US, can promise otherwise. What you can do is choose a place that takes those risks seriously.

These are the standards we hold ourselves to, and the ones we'd suggest you ask any clinic about:

  • Surgery only in an operating room. At noon, procedures are done in licensed operating rooms at Q2, our surgical facility. Never in a doctor's office.
  • An in-person consultation before anything is scheduled. Photos sent over WhatsApp are useful for sharing information. They are not a surgical evaluation.
  • Pre-op testing, with no exceptions. If your results aren't where they need to be, surgery is postponed. Even if you've already flown in.
  • Informed consent. Your surgeon walks you through the risks one by one before you sign.

Red flags apply everywhere, including with us: a quote with no breakdown, surgery outside an operating room, nobody asking for lab work, pressure to pay fast, and a doctor who says yes to everything. A good specialist also tells you what can't or shouldn't be done. Our safety page covers this in more depth (it's in Spanish).

Why so many people from the US have surgery in Colombia

According to the US Census (ACS 2024), about 1.77 million people of Colombian origin live in the United States, more than half a million of them in Florida. For many of them, Colombia is still where the family is.

For Colombian Americans, it's often about trust and comfort: talking to a surgeon in Spanish, and recovering near family. Others come for the trip itself. And cost is part of the picture too. According to the ASPS, the average surgeon's fee alone in the US is around USD 4,711 for liposuction, USD 4,875 for breast augmentation with implants and USD 8,174 for a tummy tuck (source: ASPS). Those numbers don't include anesthesia, the operating room or other costs.

noon doesn't publish prices. The cost is defined at your consultation, once your surgeon knows what you actually need. And honestly, if price is the only thing driving the decision, slow down.

Entry rules for getting plastic surgery in Colombia as a US resident

US citizens don't need a tourist visa for Colombia. Colombian immigration (Migración Colombia) grants a stay of up to 90 days, which can be extended, up to 180 days per calendar year. The regulation (Resolution 3167 of 2019) lists medical treatment as a permitted activity for tourists.

If you're a dual US-Colombian citizen, the rule is different: you must enter and leave Colombia on a valid Colombian passport. Sort this out well before you get to the airport.

Migración Colombia also recommends completing Check-MIG, a free online form, before your trip. Immigration rules change and every situation is different, so check the current requirements before you book.

Planning your dates and how long to stay

Most people plan backwards: they get a week off work, then look for a surgery that fits. It works much better the other way around.

  1. Before you fly. Message us on WhatsApp, tell us what you're considering, and we book your consultation for when you arrive.
  2. Consultation and tests. Your surgeon examines you in person and orders pre-op lab work.
  3. Surgery. If everything checks out, in the licensed operating rooms at Q2.
  4. Recovery and follow-ups. You stay in Medellin for as long as your surgeon recommends.
  5. Going home. When your surgeon clears you to fly.

As a reference: 1 to 2 weeks for face surgery, 1 to 2 weeks for breast surgery, and 2 to 3 weeks for body contouring and combined procedures. A tummy tuck plus lipo is not a one-week trip. Better to know that before you ask your manager for time off.

Book a flexible return ticket, since your flight home depends on how you heal, not on the calendar. Stay close to the clinic, too. You'll have several follow-up visits.

A practical note: Medellin's international airport (MDE) is in Rionegro, outside the city, so plan for the drive. How you get there depends on where you live. Miami has nonstop flights on American and Avianca, about 3.5 hours (surgery in Medellin from Miami). From New York, Avianca flies nonstop from JFK in roughly 5.5 hours (from New York). Houston has a United nonstop of about 4 hours 40 minutes (from Houston). From Atlanta or Boston, you'd typically fly to Bogota and connect on a short domestic flight. Don't travel alone

If you're having surgery, you need an adult with you for the first few days. Someone to help you get up, keep track of medications and get you to follow-ups. Many patients have family in Colombia who step in. Others bring a partner or a friend from home. If your companion can only stay a few days, make sure those are the days right after surgery.

Flying home and follow-up care

When can you fly? When your surgeon clears you at a follow-up visit. As a general reference, the ASPS suggests waiting 5 to 7 days before flying after body procedures like liposuction or breast augmentation, and 7 to 10 days after facial surgery. It also notes that the risk of blood clots from immobility, such as long flights, is higher in the 6 weeks after surgery. Those are general guidelines. Your actual date depends on your recovery.

Finish every follow-up you're given before leaving, and make sure you understand your home care instructions. Back in the US, we stay in touch through remote follow-up on WhatsApp or video call. It doesn't replace the follow-ups in Medellin before your flight, and anything urgent goes to the nearest emergency room. We also recommend having a doctor you trust nearby in case you need to be seen in person.
