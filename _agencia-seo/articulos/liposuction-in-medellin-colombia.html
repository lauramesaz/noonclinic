What liposuction actually does (and doesn't)

Liposuction removes localized fat that doesn't respond to diet or exercise, sculpting areas like the abdomen, waist, back, arms or thighs. It isn't a weight-loss method. It works best on people at a stable weight, close to where they want to stay, who are after shape, not a lower number on the scale.

At noon, it's done in an operating room, inside Q2's licensed surgical suites, never in a consultation office. Depending on the areas treated, it runs 2 to 5 hours under general or spinal anesthesia. Whether it's right for you, and how much it covers, gets decided by the surgeon at your in-person consultation, not on a form you fill out before booking a flight.

The question almost everyone asks: can it be combined with something else?

If you're flying in from far away, it's a reasonable thing to want: one flight, one recovery, two things handled at once. Liposuction with fat transfer to the buttocks, the most requested combination, is a real option, and it has its own page: lipotransferencia glútea (the procedure pages on our site are in Spanish, like most of noon's website; the content still applies to you). But it isn't a vacation-scheduling call. Your surgeon decides, with your pre-op tests in hand, not the number of days off your job approved.

There's a specific reason for that caution, and it's not just clinic caution for its own sake. The CDC reviewed deaths of US citizens following cosmetic surgery abroad between 2009 and 2022, and found that nearly all the fat embolism cases in that detailed review involved patients who'd had liposuction combined with fat transfer to the buttocks (BBL) in the same surgery. The CDC itself points to the risk factor as combining several long procedures in one surgery, along with obesity, not the country where it happens. That's exactly why that decision at noon runs through pre-op tests, not "you're already here, might as well." The risk isn't the country. It's how much you try to fit into one surgery.
