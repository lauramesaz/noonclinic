Thanksgiving gives you Thursday and Friday off, and the weekend tacks on by itself. Somewhere between booking flights, the question comes up: should this be the trip where you also get a procedure done in Medellín? It's a fair question, and the honest answer has less to do with how much you want it to work and more with how many real days you'll actually have in the country, which is usually fewer than you first picture.

Thanksgiving doesn't change Medellín's rhythm

Unlike December, Thanksgiving is a US holiday, not a Colombian one. On Thursday, November 26, 2026, you have the day off work; in Medellín, it's an ordinary Thursday. No school closures, no "novenas," no local family calendar eating into your recovery time, the way December's holiday season does. That part of the trip is simpler. What doesn't change is that the break itself tends to be short, and that's worth checking against real numbers before you get attached to the plan.

How much time each option actually needs

A typical Thanksgiving break, travel included, runs 4 or 5 days if Thursday and Friday are all the time off you have. That's less than almost any surgery calls for. Before booking anything, it's worth comparing your actual days off against the reference stay for each type of procedure.

Face or breast surgeryReference stay: 1 to 2 weeks
Body contouring or combined surgeryReference stay: 2 to 3 weeks
Non-surgical treatmentsUsually a single visit or a few days

Laid out that way, the problem is easy to see: even the shortest facial surgeries, like eyelid surgery, need more time than a 4- or 5-day weekend gives you. Without extra vacation days tacked onto either end of the holiday, the math simply doesn't work for most surgeries.

Why rushing it isn't worth the risk

This isn't only a calendar problem. The ASPS notes that clot risk from immobility, including a long flight, is higher during the six weeks after surgery, and that, as general guidance, patients should wait 5 to 7 days before flying after body procedures and 7 to 10 days after facial surgery. Flying home on day two or three because Monday means back to the office isn't a decision the calendar gets to make; it's your surgeon's call, based on how your follow-up visit goes, never a return date set in advance.

There's a practical layer on top of that: the Tuesday and Wednesday before Thanksgiving are historically, year after year, among the busiest air-travel days in the US, and the Sunday after the holiday too. If your route to Medellín connects through Miami or Houston, that congestion hits twice, going down and coming back, right when you have the least room for a flight change.

What actually fits in the long weekend

Non-surgical treatments are where the real room is. A treatment like Botox takes a single visit with no days of recovery built in, so it fits comfortably between Thursday's dinner and the flight home. If you're set on a facial procedure and can take 3 or 4 extra vacation days before or after the holiday, eyelid surgery can get close to working; breast surgery or body contouring, with a 1- to 3-week reference stay, almost never fits into a trip built only around Thanksgiving. If your trip runs into December

Some patients with family in Medellín stretch the Thanksgiving trip a few extra days, into late November or early December. If that's you, you may catch the start of the city's Christmas lights display, which usually goes up around that time. That's useful for planning a longer trip you're already taking, not a reason to rush a procedure your surgeon thinks needs more days than you have.
