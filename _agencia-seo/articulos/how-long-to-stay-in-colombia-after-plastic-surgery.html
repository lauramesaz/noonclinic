You've almost booked the flight to Medellín, and now comes the question that actually shapes your time off work: how long do you need to stay in Colombia after plastic surgery? There's no single number that works for everyone. It depends on the procedure, how you heal, and what your surgeon says at your follow-up. Here's what we can tell you for sure, without rounding a number to make it sound better than it is.

Short answer: 1 to 2 weeks for facial or breast surgery, 2 to 3 weeks for body contouring or a combined surgery, and the same day or a couple of days later if you're only getting a non-surgical treatment. Your surgeon confirms the exact date at your follow-up, not the ticket you already bought.

How long to stay in Colombia, by procedure

Someone who asks their boss for ten days off, planning only for lipo, comes up short if they're also getting a tummy tuck. Why your surgeon decides when you fly, not your calendar

Here's the part that matters most: these ranges are a starting point, not your flight date. Your return date is cleared by your surgeon at your follow-up, based on how your body is actually healing, not on the ticket you already bought or the days your job approved.

ASPS (the American Society of Plastic Surgeons) suggests, as a general reference, waiting 5 to 7 days before flying after body procedures like lipo or breast augmentation, and 7 to 10 days after facial surgery. It also notes that the risk of blood clots from immobility, the same concern behind any long-haul flight, stays higher for the 6 weeks following surgery.

What does that mean in practice? Buying a fixed-date return ticket before your surgery is the surest way to end up changing it. Book something flexible instead, and confirm the exact date once you're in Medellín, after your first follow-up.

No exceptions, not even for a ticket you already paid for.

How those days are organized in Medellín

A typical trip is organized like this:

  1. Before you fly. You message us on WhatsApp, tell us what you're interested in, and we schedule your consultation for when you land.
  2. Consultation and pre-op tests. Your specialist examines you in person and, for surgery, orders the required tests. No one operates on a patient they haven't examined in person.
  3. Your procedure. Surgery is performed in Q2's licensed operating rooms, never in a doctor's office.
  4. Follow-ups. Several check-ins over the following days to track healing and swelling.
  5. Clearance to fly. Your surgeon confirms when you can get on a plane.

That window, between your consultation and your clearance to fly, is where these one-to-three weeks actually happen. A combined surgery doesn't just add recovery time: it adds follow-up visits, and each one needs its own appointment.

Where you stay, and why it matters

If you're having surgery, you need an adult with you for the first few days. That's not a formality: it's someone who helps you get up, reminds you about medication, and gets you to follow-ups. It can be family you already have in Colombia or someone who travels with you from the US.

Stay close to the clinic. It sounds like a minor detail until you're moving through Medellín traffic in a compression garment for a fifteen-minute check-up. We go into more detail in where to stay in Medellín during your recovery. One more thing worth knowing: Medellín's airport, José María Córdova (MDE), is actually in Rionegro, outside the city, so factor that drive into your first and last day.

Flying in from the US: time, flights and entry rules

Colombia runs on its own time zone year-round (UTC-5, no daylight saving), so if you're coming from Miami, New York, Boston, Atlanta or Orlando, the clocks match in winter and you're an hour ahead of Medellín in summer; from Houston, it's the reverse. Nonstop flights exist from several of these cities, and we break those down city by city, along with what else to plan, in getting plastic surgery in Colombia as a US resident.

US citizens don't need a tourism visa: Colombian immigration grants a permit of up to 90 days, extendable up to 180 days a year, and that rule covers medical treatment as a permitted tourist activity. If you hold dual citizenship, you enter and exit Colombia on your valid Colombian passport. Immigration also recommends the free, online Check-MIG before you travel. None of this is personalized immigration advice, so check current requirements before you book.
