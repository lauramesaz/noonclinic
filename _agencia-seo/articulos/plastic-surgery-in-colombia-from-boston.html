What makes surgery in Medellin safe, or not

Safety doesn't depend on the country. It depends on the clinic, the operating room and the process. At noon Clinic, all surgery happens in the operating rooms at Q2, never in an office or a consultation room. You're seen in person before anything is decided: WhatsApp is for information, not approvals. Lab work comes first, and if your results aren't right, surgery is postponed. Before you sign the informed consent, we explain the risks one by one: bleeding, infection, healing problems and anesthesia-related complications, among others. And you don't fly home until your surgeon clears you at a follow-up visit.

We'll also tell you when something isn't a good idea for you. If a clinic says yes to everything, that's a red flag. Our safety page (in Spanish) lists six warning signs worth checking anywhere, including with us.

The route: Boston to Medellin

There's no nonstop from Logan to Medellin today. Your realistic options:

  • Avianca, Boston to Bogota nonstop, then a short domestic flight from Bogota to Medellin.
  • Through New York. Avianca flies JFK to Medellin nonstop, roughly 5 h 30 min.
  • Through Miami. American and Avianca fly nonstop from Miami to Medellin, roughly 3 h 30 min.

Times are approximate and schedules change. Whatever you choose, ask the airline what happens to your connection if the first flight is delayed. That matters more on the way home, when you're a week or two out of surgery.

Something most first-timers don't know: Medellin's international airport, José María Córdova (MDE), is in Rionegro, outside the city. Budget time for the drive in.

How long to stay

A common mistake when you travel this far is booking the return too early. Our reference ranges for patients coming from out of town:

For rhinoplasty or other face surgery, plan on 1 to 2 weeks in Medellin. Breast surgery, such as breast augmentation, is also 1 to 2 weeks. Body contouring like liposuction, or combined procedures, 2 to 3 weeks. Non-surgical treatments usually take the same day or a few days.

Procedure pages are in Spanish for now. Your return date depends on how you heal, so buy a flexible ticket.

Flying home to a Boston winter

The trip back has at least one connection, which means more time seated. The American Society of Plastic Surgeons notes that clot risk from immobility, including long flights, is higher during the six weeks after surgery. As general background, the ASPS suggests waiting 5 to 7 days before flying after body procedures and 7 to 10 days after facial surgery. At noon, your surgeon makes that call at your follow-up visit, based on how you're doing.

Then you land in the cold. A few practical things:

  • Pack a coat that's easy to get on and off without lifting your arms much, especially after breast surgery.
  • Icy sidewalks. For the first weeks, have someone go with you on errands.
  • Know your aftercare plan. Before leaving Medellin, make sure you understand your home care instructions and what to do if something worries you.
  • Don't go straight back to work. Give yourself a day or two at home after the flight.

Why plan this for winter? Partly the obvious: Medellin has mild weather year-round. But also the practical side. You're indoors more, a heavy coat covers a compression garment, and fewer people ask why you're skipping the beach. If you're aiming for December, book early and protect your recovery days from holiday plans.

Boston's Colombian community

About 43,000 people of Colombian origin live in the Boston area, roughly 18,000 of them in Suffolk County, according to the Census Bureau. East Boston is one of the neighborhoods best known for its Colombian presence. If you grew up in a Colombian family there, you may know someone who had surgery back home. That's a good starting point for questions. It's not a substitute for your own consultation.
