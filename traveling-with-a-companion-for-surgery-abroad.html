    Travel and companion guide

    Traveling with a companion
    for surgery in Colombia

    This isn't about having company on the flight. It's about having someone ready to help when you can't.

    Guides · noon Clinic team · Updated October 7, 2026

    You've picked the date, the flight to Medellin, even where you're staying. The part a lot of patients leave for last, almost as an afterthought, is who they're traveling with. It shouldn't be last: traveling with a companion for surgery abroad shapes what your first days after the procedure actually look like, not just how the flight down feels.

    Why traveling with a companion for surgery abroad isn't optional

    In the hours right after you're out of surgery, your body doesn't do the things it normally does without thinking. Sitting up, standing, pulling on a compression garment, zipping a suitcase: all of it can take more out of you than you'd expect. That's why, if your procedure is surgical, we ask you to travel with an adult who's with you for at least the first days.

    It's not paperwork or a clinic formality. That person is who our team contacts the moment you're out of surgery, who gets you to your follow-up visits, and who's there if anything doesn't go the way it should. For non-surgical treatments, where there's no operating room or general anesthesia, we usually don't ask for one.

    Who qualifies, and what a companion can't fix

    It doesn't have to be your mother or your spouse. A friend, a sibling, anyone you're comfortable being vulnerable around for a few days, all of that works. What actually matters isn't the relationship on paper, it's whether that person can be available, without other commitments pulling them out of the apartment right when you need them most.

    And here's a limit worth stating plainly: your companion isn't medical staff. They don't make decisions for you, they don't replace your surgeon at a follow-up visit, and they can't manage a complication: that's what noon's team is for. If something falls outside the expected, it's the emergency room wherever you happen to be. Their job is simpler than that, and still the one that matters most: showing up.

    What your companion actually does in Medellin, day by day

    On surgery day, they wait at or near the clinic and are your contact while you're in the operating room. The days after, the job shifts: helping you shower without straining your abdomen or arms, carrying what you shouldn't, getting you to follow-up appointments, and often just noticing things you miss because you're focused on not moving wrong.

    Between appointments, once you can move around a bit more, your companion has free hours too. Medellin gives them plenty to do with that time: a coffee in El Poblado, an afternoon in Comuna 13, a market downtown. They don't need to spend those hours sitting around waiting for you, just be reachable when you actually need them.

    When you both want to make the most of the trip

    It comes up a lot: your companion has been thinking about getting something done too, and the trip looks like the perfect chance for both of you. That's fair, but there's something worth talking through before you book: if you're both recovering at the same time, neither of you can really take care of the other. Someone needs to be well enough to help the other get dressed or get to a follow-up, and that can't be the person who was also just in surgery that same week.

    If you're both genuinely interested, staggering the dates works better than stacking them. Tell us what you're both considering before you plan the trip: we'll walk you through what order makes sense given each procedure.

    If no one can come with you

    We know it's not a small ask, asking someone for a week or two of their life, especially if they're in Houston or Boston juggling their own job and family. If you've read this far without a clear answer on who can travel with you, hold off on booking: message us on WhatsApp, and based on your procedure, we'll tell you what's realistic.

    What to do this week

    If you already know who's coming, confirm the tentative dates with them and check that their passport is valid. If you don't know yet, settle it before you buy flights: it's far easier to change a date than to travel without the support your recovery actually needs. It's also worth reading how long to stay in Colombia after plastic surgery and where to stay in Medellin to recover after surgery, keeping in mind that the space and the time both need to work for two.

    Frequently asked questions

    Frequently asked questions

    Do I have to travel with a companion?

    If your procedure is surgery, yes: you need an adult with you for at least the first days. For non-surgical treatments, usually not.

    Does my companion have to be family?

    No. A partner, a friend, a sibling: what matters is that they're an adult who can actually be there, not how they're related to you.

    Does my companion need a visa to enter Colombia?

    If they're a US citizen, no: they enter on the same tourist permit you do, up to 90 days. If they hold Colombian citizenship too, they need to travel in and out on their valid Colombian passport.

    What does my companion actually do while I'm in surgery?

    They wait at or near the clinic and are the contact person once you're out. For the days after, they help with what your body can't do yet: getting dressed, moving around, getting to follow-up visits.

    Can my companion get a procedure done on the same trip?

    They can, but think it through first: if you're both recovering at the same time, neither of you can really take care of the other. If you're both interested, talk to us about timing before you book the trip.

    What if no one can travel with me?

    Message us before you book. Depending on your procedure, we'll tell you honestly whether that's workable or whether you really need someone with you.
