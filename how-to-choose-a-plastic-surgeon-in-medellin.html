    How to choose a surgeon
    in Medellin, not just a clinic

    A clinic's name on Instagram tells you nothing about who's actually holding the scalpel.

    International patients · noon Clinic team · Updated October 9, 2026

    Search how to choose a plastic surgeon in Medellin and you mostly get two things: polished before-and-after reels and clinics calling themselves "the best" without saying why. Neither tells you whether the person who'd actually operate on you holds a real plastic surgery specialty. That's not something a filtered photo can answer. It's something you can check, with a name and a few minutes, before you book a flight.

    What matters first, and it isn't the clinic's logo

    In Colombia, becoming a plastic surgeon requires a medical degree followed by a formal specialization in plastic, aesthetic and reconstructive surgery through a university program approved by the Colombian government. That specialty then gets registered with a national health authority. It's not a nice-to-have credential. It's the line between a surgeon and someone who picked up a technique in a weekend course.

    RETHUSColombia's Ministry of Health registry; confirms a doctor's actual specialty
    SCCPPrivate surgeons' association; not a government certifier
    REPSRegistry of facilities licensed for surgery, not of individual doctors

    How to choose a plastic surgeon in Medellin, in practice

    You don't need a law degree or a medical one for this. You need the surgeon's full name and a little patience before you decide.

    • Ask for the surgeon's full legal name, not just the clinic's brand. With that, you can look them up in RETHUS and confirm the plastic surgery specialty is actually registered to them, not just implied by a title on a website.
    • Check whether they're listed with the SCCP (Sociedad Colombiana de Cirugía Plástica), the country's professional association for plastic surgeons. It's a useful signal of peer standing, but the SCCP itself is clear that it's a private group, not a state certifier, so it doesn't replace RETHUS.
    • Confirm the facility where they operate is registered in REPS, Colombia's registry of licensed healthcare facilities. A well-credentialed surgeon can still operate somewhere that shouldn't be doing surgery.
    • Ask who actually examines you at your consultation. If the answer isn't, by name, the same person who signs your informed consent and operates, keep asking.

    Talk to the person who'll operate, not just the one selling you on it

    It's normal for a clinic to have a coordinator who handles scheduling, answers WhatsApp messages and walks you through logistics while you're still deciding. That part's fine. The problem is when that same person is effectively the one "clearing" you for surgery, without the surgeon ever examining you.

    Questions worth asking at the consultation

    These apply at noon or at any clinic in Medellin you're weighing. If one of these makes someone uncomfortable, that's an answer too.

    • What's your specialty registration, and where did you train?
    • Where exactly do you operate, and is that facility licensed for surgery?
    • Who examines me at the consultation: you, or someone from the sales team?
    • What happens if my pre-op labs come back abnormal?
    • Who handles it if something doesn't go as expected, in Colombia or once I'm back home?

    What this checklist can't do for you

    Verifying a registration tells you the surgeon is who they claim to be. It doesn't tell you they're the right surgeon for your specific case, and that only gets answered in person, looking at your history and your labs, not on a web form or a five-minute call. If anything you read here, or anywhere else, doesn't match what you're being told over WhatsApp, ask again before you buy the ticket, not after.

    Frequently asked questions

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I choose a plastic surgeon in Medellin from the US?

    Get the surgeon's full legal name and look it up in RETHUS, Colombia's national health workforce registry, to confirm the plastic surgery specialty is actually registered to them. Check whether they're listed with the SCCP, the surgeons' professional association. Confirm the facility is licensed in REPS. And make sure the person examining you at your consultation is the same person who'll operate, not a sales rep.

    What is RETHUS?

    RETHUS (Registro Único Nacional del Talento Humano en Salud) is Colombia's Ministry of Health registry for healthcare professionals. You search a doctor's name and see which medical specialty is officially registered to them.

    Does the Colombian Society of Plastic Surgery certify surgeons?

    No. The SCCP is a private professional association, not a government body. It screens members against its own membership requirements, which is a useful reference, but it doesn't replace checking RETHUS.

    Can my first consultation happen over WhatsApp or video call?

    WhatsApp is fine for questions and scheduling, but the actual consultation is always in person. No surgeon worth trusting signs off on a procedure without examining you face to face first.

    What if the person who evaluates me isn't the surgeon who'll operate?

    Ask why. A coordinator handling logistics and your questions is normal. Deciding the surgical plan and reviewing your case is the surgeon's job, not a salesperson's.
