    Before you decide

    All about nonsurgical double chin treatment

    What nonsurgical double chin treatment is and what else it's called

    You may see it called double chin treatment, double chin removal without surgery or nonsurgical chin lipo, and in Spanish, papada sin cirugía. It isn't a single procedure: it's a set of aesthetic medicine options, chosen based on what's behind your double chin. According to the American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS), it can come from genetics, excess fat or loose skin. At noon Clinic in Medellín, the options are cryolipolysis (in Spanish) for fat, and HIFU or radiofrequency for mild sagging. The surgical option is chin liposuction.

    How it works, and how it differs from chin liposuction

    None of the three involves incisions or time off work. With cryolipolysis, according to the FDA, an applicator uses suction to draw in the fat you can pinch and cools it; your body clears it, usually within two to three months. In the United States, the FDA has cleared at least one cryolipolysis device for fat under the chin and one HIFU device for loose tissue in that area. HIFU and radiofrequency heat deep layers of the skin: they work on firmness, not fat. Chin liposuction is surgery, in an operating room, with 5 to 7 days off work.

    Who it's not for

    These options aren't a good fit if:

    • You have a lot of loose skin: according to the AAD, tightening may not help, and the ASPS says cryolipolysis may not be right for you.
    • You want to lose weight: the FDA notes that nonsurgical body contouring doesn't treat obesity.
    • You're pregnant, or you're breastfeeding and considering cryolipolysis.
    • You have cold urticaria, cryoglobulinemia, Raynaud's or another cold sensitivity condition, or poor circulation in the area: the FDA advises against cryolipolysis.
    • You have a pacemaker or another active implant, or metal in the area: HIFU and radiofrequency aren't used in that case.
    Risks you should know about

    Every procedure has risks. For cryolipolysis, the FDA lists, among others:

    • Redness, bruising, swelling, pain or skin discoloration, which usually fade on their own.
    • Numbness that can last several weeks, nodules or freeze burns, which can be severe.
    • Under the chin, a temporary feeling of fullness at the back of the throat; under the jawline, nerve damage that temporarily changes the position of the lip or tongue.
    • Rarely, the fat grows instead of shrinking; it doesn't resolve on its own and may need surgery.

    With HIFU and radiofrequency, the AAD says the most common effects are temporary redness and swelling.

    How to prepare and what to expect afterward

    For your consultation, bring a list of what you take, including supplements, and tell your doctor if you're pregnant or breastfeeding, if you have implants and what you've had done on your neck or chin. You go back to your routine right away; the area may be red, swollen, tender or numb for a few days. With cryolipolysis, the AAD says you may notice changes as early as 3 weeks. With HIFU, the effect builds over 2 to 3 months. Radiofrequency usually takes 4 to 8 sessions. If you notice blisters, a burn or the area getting bigger, message us.

    When another option makes more sense

    If we see at your consultation that what you need goes beyond what these options can do, we'll tell you:

    • A lot of fat, or you'd rather remove it in a single surgery: chin liposuction, with Dr. Fernando Ruiz, a plastic surgeon.
    • Significant loose skin on the neck: a neck lift. According to the ASPS, nonsurgical tightening doesn't match the improvement in sagging that surgery achieves.
    • A chin with little projection, which makes a double chin more noticeable: hyaluronic acid fillers or chin surgery.
    • Losing weight: medical weight management.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    HIFU or cryolipolysis: which is better for a double chin?

    They treat different things: cryolipolysis works on fat, and HIFU on mild sagging. Your specialist decides which one suits you at your in-person consultation.

    Do I need time off work?

    No. You go back to your routine as soon as you leave; the area may be red, swollen, tender or numb for a few days.

    Can you get rid of a double chin without surgery?

    It depends on what's causing it. If it's fat you can pinch, cryolipolysis can reduce it over time; if it's mild sagging, HIFU or radiofrequency work on firmness. An ASPS member surgeon explains that nonsurgical options can help reduce fat under the chin in carefully selected candidates, though results are more gradual. If there's a lot of fat or significant loose skin, we'll evaluate you for chin liposuction or a neck lift. Your specialist will tell you which applies to you at your in-person consultation.

    Does cryolipolysis work on a double chin?

    It can, when your double chin is fat you can pinch: in the United States, the FDA has cleared at least one cryolipolysis device for fat under the chin. Results are gradual: according to the AAD, you may notice changes as early as 3 weeks, and according to the FDA, your body usually clears the treated fat within two to three months. If loose skin is what stands out most, the ASPS says you may not be a good candidate.

    Will losing weight get rid of my double chin?

    Not always. According to the ASPS, a double chin can come from genetics, excess fat or loose skin, so it isn't only about weight. And nonsurgical treatments aren't meant for weight loss: according to the AAD, good candidates have a small bulge of fat that remains despite diet and exercise, and a weight in the normal range for their height. If your goal is to lose weight, that's what medical weight management is for.

    Are “enzyme” injections for a double chin safe?

    First, find out exactly what you'd be getting. INVIMA, Colombia's national regulator for medicines and health products, has warned about products sold as “fat-burning enzymes” without sanitary registration, which are illegal to sell in Colombia. And the FDA has received reports of permanent scarring, serious infections and deep, painful knots from fat-dissolving injections it has not approved. Before you let anyone give you a product, ask what it is and look up its sanitary registration on INVIMA's website.
