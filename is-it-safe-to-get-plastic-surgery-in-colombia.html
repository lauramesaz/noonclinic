    Safety and surgery abroad

    Is it safe
    to get plastic surgery in Colombia?

    Short answer: it can be, and what decides that is the clinic, not the country.

    International patients · noon Clinic team · Updated October 8, 2026

    If you're searching whether it's safe to get plastic surgery in Colombia, you've probably already read something that made you pause: a horror story on social media, a comment in a Facebook group, a relative who used the words "medical tourism" like a warning. It's a fair question, and the short answer is yes: surgery in Colombia can be as safe as surgery in Houston or Miami. What decides that isn't the country. It's whether the clinic you pick, here or there, actually meets concrete standards.

    What actually makes it safe to get plastic surgery in Colombia

    The stories that go viral almost never mention a country by accident. They mention a procedure done outside an operating room, no pre-op labs, no one explaining the risks before a signature. That's what failed, not the fact that it happened abroad. A clinic you can trust, in Medellín or in Texas, rests on the same basics: where they operate, who examines you first, and what they put in writing.

    Operating roomSurgery only in a licensed facility, never a doctor's office
    ConsultationIn person, before anything gets scheduled
    Informed consentRisks explained one by one, in writing, before you sign

    How we do it at noon, in Medellín

    At noon, surgery happens in the licensed operating rooms at Q2, never in an office. Before that, Dr. Fernando Ruiz or Dr. Valeria Enciso examines you in person. WhatsApp is for questions and scheduling; the actual consultation is always in person. If your pre-op labs aren't in order, surgery gets postponed, no exceptions.

    Once you're back home, we keep checking in by WhatsApp or video call. That has a limit worth stating plainly: it doesn't replace the in-person follow-up visits your surgeon schedules before you fly, and a real emergency means your local emergency room, not waiting on a video call.

    One logistics detail that catches first-time visitors off guard: Medellín's international airport, José María Córdova (MDE), isn't actually in Medellín. It's in Rionegro, a separate town, with a drive of close to an hour into the city. It has nothing to do with safety on its own, but it's the kind of thing a clinic that knows what it's doing tells you before you land, not after you're standing at the curb confused about where your ride is.

    Six red flags, wherever you're considering

    These hold true in Medellín, in Miami, or at any clinic you're weighing, ours included. Ask without hesitating.

    • No written, itemized quote. Whatever isn't spelled out upfront tends to show up later as an extra charge.
    • No real operating room. Surgery belongs in a surgical facility, not a converted office.
    • No one asks for labs. Without pre-op testing, no one's actually checking whether it's safe to operate on you.
    • No informed consent. You're entitled to know the risks before you decide, not after.
    • Everything is "yes." A surgeon worth trusting also tells you what shouldn't be done.
    • Pressure to pay fast. A decision like this deserves time, not urgency.

    What no honest clinic will promise you

    Every surgery carries risk: bleeding, infection, healing problems, anesthesia complications, among others. If anyone tells you "risk-free" or guarantees a result, that's your first red flag. The ASPS, the American Society of Plastic Surgeons, notes that the risk of blood clots from immobility, including on a long flight, is higher for six weeks after surgery. That's why your surgeon decides your return flight date at a follow-up visit, not a booking you made before you ever got examined.

    And here's something worth saying plainly: sometimes the right answer is that you shouldn't have surgery, or not yet. If your labs aren't clear or a procedure doesn't make sense for your case, we'll tell you at noon, even if it costs us the booking.

    Before you book, ask this

    Whatever clinic you're considering, these questions tell you more than any review online.

    • Exactly where does the surgery happen, and can they confirm that facility is licensed?
    • Who examines me in person before anything is scheduled, and when?
    • What pre-op labs will I need, and what happens if something isn't clear?
    • Who handles it if something doesn't go as expected, in Colombia or once I'm back home?

    Ask these same questions on our safety page (in Spanish), with us or with any other clinic on your list.     Frequently asked questions

    Frequently asked questions

    Is it safe to get plastic surgery in Colombia?

    It can be, same as anywhere, if the clinic meets specific standards: a licensed operating room, an in-person consultation, required pre-op labs and informed consent. The risk isn't about the country. It's about the clinic.

    Are operating rooms in Colombia regulated?

    Yes. Licensed surgical facilities exist, like the ones at Q2 where we operate. Ask any clinic exactly where they perform surgery, and be wary if they can't answer clearly.

    Is it legal for a US citizen to have surgery in Colombia?

    Yes. As a tourist, US citizens get an entry permit of up to 90 days, extendable, and Colombian immigration rules list medical treatment among the activities that permit covers.

    What if I have a complication once I'm back home?

    An emergency means an emergency room, wherever you are. We stay in touch by WhatsApp or video call, but that follow-up doesn't replace in-person emergency care.

    Can I get approved for surgery without an in-person exam?

    No, and you shouldn't want a clinic that offers that. WhatsApp is for questions and scheduling. No serious clinic operates on you without examining you in person first.
