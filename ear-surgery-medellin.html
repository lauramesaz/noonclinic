    Before you decide

    All about otoplasty

    Otoplasty, ear pinning and ears that stick out

    Otoplasty is surgery on the ears. Many people search for it as “ear pinning,” “ears that stick out” or “protruding ears.” According to the American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS), it improves the shape, position and proportion of the ear. It is used for ears that stick out, on one side or both; for very large ears (macrotia); and for adults who were not happy with a previous ear surgery. The ASPS points out that prominent ears are not linked to hearing loss.

    How the ear is corrected

    The incision usually goes on the back of the ear; if one is needed in front, it is hidden in the ear’s folds. According to the ASPS, in ears that stick out, the fold that runs just inside the rim (the antihelix) is created or made more defined and, if the cartilage of the bowl of the ear (the concha) is enlarged, it is reduced. Internal stitches, which are not removed, hold the cartilage in its new shape. There is no single technique for everyone: your surgeon chooses it after examining you. If you also want to change your nose or your chin, mention it at your consultation; your surgeon decides whether it makes sense to do it in the same surgery.

    Children, teens and adults: when to wait

    It is done in adults and in children from about age 6. According to the ASPS, the person having surgery should be healthy and, if they are a teen or an adult, should not smoke. It is better to wait, and talk it over with your surgeon, if:

    • There are untreated chronic ear infections.
    • There is a medical condition that makes healing harder.
    • The child objects when the surgery comes up, or can’t follow instructions well yet.
    • The goal is to please someone else, not yourself.
    Risks you should know about

    Every surgery has risks, and this one does too. The ASPS lists, among others:

    • Anesthesia risks.
    • Asymmetry.
    • Bleeding (hematoma) and blood clots.
    • Infection.
    • Changes in skin sensation.
    • Pain that may persist.
    • Poor wound healing or unfavorable scarring.
    • Skin discoloration, swelling or irregularities.
    • Allergy to tape, sutures or other products used.
    • Possible need for revision surgery.

    Before you decide, we go over each one with you, and you sign an informed consent form.

    How to prepare

    The surgery is done in the licensed operating rooms at Q2, never in a doctor’s office, and you need pre-op tests first. The ASPS also advises quitting smoking well ahead of time and avoiding aspirin, certain anti-inflammatory drugs and herbal supplements, because they can increase bleeding. Don’t stop or change any medication on your own: go over everything you take with your specialist. Arrange for someone to take you home and stay with you at least the first night. You’ll find the step-by-step process on our international patients page.

    Aftercare: leave the bandage alone

    For the first week you wear a bandage around your head and, from week 2 to week 6, a headband to sleep in. According to the ASPS, do not take the bandage off on your own for any reason: you could lose part of the correction and need another surgery. It may itch underneath. While you heal, protect the incisions from bumps, rubbing, sun and tanning beds. You get clear instructions, pain relievers for the discomfort of the first few days and follow-up visits until you are discharged. If you notice anything that worries you, let your surgeon know.

    Coming from abroad

    Plan your trip.

    If you are flying in, plan to stay in Medellín for about 1 to 2 weeks for this surgery, and fly home only when your surgeon clears you. Travel with an adult companion for the first days.

    FAQ

    Common questions

    Does it hurt?

    There is some discomfort for the first few days, managed with pain relievers.

    At what age can otoplasty be done?

    In children from about age 6, and also in teens and adults. Age is not the only factor: the child also needs to be healthy, cooperate and not object to the surgery. Your surgeon confirms this at your in-person consultation.

    Will the otoplasty scar show?

    The incision is on the back of the ear and, according to the ASPS, the scar usually stays hidden behind the ear or in its natural creases. Everyone's skin heals differently; while it heals, protect the area from sun and rubbing as your specialist directs.

    Can my ears stick out again after otoplasty?

    According to the ASPS, the ear is permanently set closer to the head. To protect that change, don't take the bandage off on your own, because you could lose part of the correction. As with any surgery, a revision is sometimes needed.

    How much time off work do I need after otoplasty?

    Plan on about 7 days off work. For the first week you wear a bandage around your head, and after that a headband to sleep in until week 6. The result looks stable between the first and third month.
